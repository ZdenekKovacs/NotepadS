NotepadS Help

Syntax highlighting

Colors for code and data files.

NotepadS colors the text based on the file’s language. The language is detected from the file name or extension, or from a #! line such as #!/usr/bin/env python3. The status bar shows it; click it to choose another language for this window (languages are grouped by their first letter).

A .m file is Objective-C when its first line starts with #, //, /* or @, and MATLAB otherwise. AutoLISP files (.lsp) use the Lisp colors; Delphi projects (.dpr) use Pascal.

The colors follow Light and Dark mode. For very large files (over 10 MB) or files with extremely long lines, highlighting turns off to keep editing fast; the status bar then says (highlighting off).

Your own languages

For a file format NotepadS doesn’t know, define a language in NotepadS › Settings… › Languages (or choose Define Your Own Language… at the bottom of the status bar’s language menu): click +, give it a name and its file extensions, and fill in what it has:

  • Line comment (such as # or //) and block comment start and end (such as /* and */);
  • String quotes (such as " and '; a string ends at the end of its line) and the escape character (such as \);
  • up to four keyword groups, each with its own color, words separated by spaces or line breaks. Words may contain symbols, such as #define or @media;
  • whether upper and lower case are the same (IF = if), and whether numbers are colored.

Changes show at once in open documents. Files with the language’s extensions are colored with it, also when a built-in language uses the same extension. Your languages are listed under My Languages in the status bar and in the Open and Save windows. Export All… saves them to a file, and Import… adds the languages from such a file, for example on another Mac. Restore Defaults doesn’t delete them.
